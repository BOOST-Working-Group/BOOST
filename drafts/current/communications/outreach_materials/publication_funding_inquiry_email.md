## Email Draft (Balanced Professional Tone)

**Subject:** BOOST Publication Initiative - Request for Publication Support

Dear Elizabeth, Michael, and Anthy,

I hope this email finds you well. I'm writing to update you on the BOOST data standard and to inquire about potential support from the Department of Conservation and the Governor's Office of Land Use and Climate Innovation for academic publication.

We have completed development of BOOST v3.4.2, a 36-entity data standard for biomass traceability from harvest through processing to biofuel production. The standard addresses regulatory compliance needs (LCFS, RFS, EU RED-II) while maintaining practical implementability for supply chain operators.

We are now ready to pursue formal academic publication. Based on research of publication venues, we have identified a dual-track strategy targeting Scientific Data (Nature Portfolio, Impact Factor: 6.9) for the data standard and validation framework, with an expected timeline of 16 weeks to publication, and the Journal of Open Source Software (JOSS) for the Python validation framework, with an expected timeline of 12 weeks and no publication fees. The Scientific Data open access format ensures broad visibility and adoption.

We are seeing interest in BOOST from several stakeholders. Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory has expressed interest in potential collaboration on the standard and its applications for biomass supply chain tracking. I am planning to join the LIAMIST interagency working group for Phase 2 of that project based on discussions with Dan Sanches at UC Davis, though meeting schedules have not yet been finalized. Recent stakeholder engagement sessions with IOUs and certification bodies have validated BOOST's approach.

To move forward with publication, I am seeking support totaling $22,290, which includes open access publication fees of $2,290 for Scientific Data (JOSS has no fees), and $20,000 for manuscript preparation and publication management services that I would provide as a sole proprietor consultant. This approach leverages my expertise in the standard's development and my established publication record while incurring very low overhead and delivering high value.

My qualifications for serving as managing author include: authorship of the 88-page BOOST specification and development of all 36 entity schemas; 30+ peer-reviewed publications in bioenergy, remote sensing, and supply chain optimization demonstrating sustained technical writing experience; lead authorship of the VCS-approved methodology for forest biomass measurement (2015), demonstrating experience navigating rigorous scientific review and formal approval processes for technical specifications; and Ph.D. research focused on biomass supply chain analysis with dissertation published in peer-reviewed venues.

The work includes manuscript drafting and literature review, co-author coordination with W3C Community Group contributors, supplementary materials preparation, peer review response and revision, stakeholder communication and dissemination, and overall publication process management.

Publishing BOOST in peer-reviewed journals delivers several benefits. Academic validation enhances adoption by industry and regulatory bodies, while Nature Portfolio reaches global sustainability and data science communities. The publication positions California's work in biomass traceability innovation, demonstrates outcomes from DOC funding investment, strengthens future grant applications and partnerships, and facilitates regulatory framework adoption through peer-reviewed publication.

If funding support is confirmed, manuscript preparation and submission can proceed in October-November 2025, followed by the peer review process in December 2025 through February 2026, with a target publication date of March 2026 and dissemination to California agencies, biomass industry, and academic community in Spring 2026.

I would appreciate the opportunity to discuss whether publication support can be accommodated within existing DOC grant budgets, interest from the Governor's Office in supporting this work as part of California's climate innovation portfolio, potential connections to other state initiatives such as LIAMIST, biomass working groups, and LCFS implementation, and opportunities to leverage LLNL's expressed interest in collaboration. I'm happy to provide additional details on the publication plan, BOOST's technical specifications, or projected outcomes. Would either of you have availability for a brief call in the coming week to discuss this further?

Thank you for your consideration and continued support of the BOOST initiative.

Best regards,

Peter Tittmann
Chair, Biomass Open Origin Standard for Tracking (BOOST) Working Group
