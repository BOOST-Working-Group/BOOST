#+TITLE: BOOST Rename — Decision Framework
#+AUTHOR: Peter Tittmann
#+DATE: <2026-05-15 Fri>
#+STATUS: For BOOST WG discussion — issue #299 (runs parallel to charter scope amendment #283)
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

* Context

The BOOST charter is being amended (issue #283) to extend scope from "solid
biomass for energy applications" to include timber and forest fiber. Independent
of the charter work, stakeholder feedback points to a possible name change.

Two distinct resistance patterns are mixed in what we're hearing:

- *Scope-level.* "I don't see myself in the current charter." Addressed by the
  charter amendment itself — naming constituencies explicitly (woody biomass for
  energy, sawn timber and engineered wood, forest fiber for pulp/paper/bio-based
  manufacturing) in the scope statement.

- *Brand-level.* "BOOST sounds like an energy thing, not a wood thing." Charter
  language doesn't reach this — in conversation people still expand BOOST as
  "Biomass…" regardless of what the document says.

This memo lays out the rename decision space so it can be discussed and
narrowed.

* Decision criteria

Any candidate should be evaluated against these:

1. *Technical credibility.* Sounds like a spec, not a startup. State agency
   staff (CARB, CEC, USFS) should not roll their eyes when typing it into a
   document.
2. *Three-constituency fit.* Works for (a) woody biomass for energy/electricity
   feedstock, (b) forest fiber and wood-products traceability,
   (c) chain-of-custody alignment under GTF umbrella positioning.
3. *Conflict landscape.* No collision with adjacent traceability standards,
   forestry ERPs, certification programs, or CoC-certified manufacturers in
   the PEFC/FSC ecosystem.
4. *Brand recognition cost.* What does the working group give up by walking
   away from "BOOST" in policy circles where it's already known.
5. *Operational cost.* Domain transitions, GitHub org migration, W3C CG rename
   process, charter republication.

* Candidates

** Path A — Retain "BOOST", re-expand the acronym

Brand stays. The "B" expands to something inclusive of all three
constituencies.

- *BOOST — Biogenic Open Origin Standard for Traceability.* "Biogenic" subsumes
  biomass-for-energy, wood products, and forest fiber. Sounds appropriately
  technical.
- *BOOST — Bio-based Open Origin Standard for Traceability.* Similar effect,
  slightly less scientific tone.
- *BOOST — Bioeconomy Open Origin Standard for Traceability.* Broadest framing;
  positions the standard as the data layer for the bio-economy.

Cost: marginal (README + charter language). Risk: doesn't reach brand-level
objectors who hear "BOOST" itself as energy-coded.

** Path B — Descriptive name

Replaces "BOOST" with a name that names the artifact directly. Two
sub-patterns are worth distinguishing because they signal different
governance postures.

*** Path B1 — GTF-X pattern (WWF sibling positioning)

Mirrors WWF's existing /Global Traceability Framework for X/ naming —
explicit, descriptive, no acronym wordplay. Reads as a sibling spec to
GTFBL (Beef & Leather) and GDST (Seafood) under the GTF umbrella.

- *GTFT — Global Traceability Framework for Timber.* Strongest GTF
  alignment. "Timber" reads as forest-products-and-fiber to industry,
  but may be heard as excluding energy biomass.
- *GTFW — Global Traceability Framework for Wood.* "Wood" is broader
  than timber in everyday usage and more obviously covers fuel-grade
  material.
- *GTFF — Global Traceability Framework for Forests.* Most inclusive;
  reads as the umbrella for everything forest-derived (timber, fiber,
  biomass).

Cost: full rename. Risk: positions BOOST as a sub-component of the
WWF/GTF brand. Inseparable from the sponsor framework's identity —
which conflates the open standard with a single-sponsor brand in the
exact way W3C CG open-standard governance is built to avoid.

*** Path B2 — Plain descriptive, framework-agnostic

Names what the artifact is in industry-vocabulary terms without
attaching to any sponsor framework. Strips out "Global" and
"Traceability Framework" — length without much signal — in favor of
literal precision.

- *Data Standard for Wood (DSW).* Plain descriptive, no acronym
  wordplay. Reads thin — sounds like a working title rather than a
  standard name.
- *Forest Products Data Standard (FPDS).* Industry-vocabulary-aligned;
  "forest products" is a recognized commodity category. Doesn't signal
  openness.
- *Open Forest Data Standard (OFDS).* Foregrounds "Open" — the
  open-standard governance posture is visible in the name itself.
  "Forest" is the most inclusive scope token (covers timber, fiber,
  and forest-derived biomass).

Cost: full rename. Risk: less brand-recognition gain from a sponsor
framework's pre-existing audience; trades brand association for
governance independence. The right risk profile if open-standard
integrity is a discriminator vs. proprietary vendor platforms (FSC
Trace, TRACT, Chainparency, LiveEO TradeAware, et al.).

** Path C — Pronounceable acronym (new brand, technical sober)

Replaces BOOST with a new short acronym that's still pronounceable and reads
as a spec name.

- *FORTE — Forest Open Records for Traceability & Exchange.* Cleanest
  pronunciation and letter-to-word mapping of what we explored. Conflicts:
  Forté NZ (PEFC CoC-certified timber flooring company) is the live risk —
  same stakeholder community. Innsbruck "FORTE" forest sensor project (2025)
  is a peripheral conflict. Domains and GitHub orgs available.
- *TRACS — Traceability Records & Attribution for Commodity Standards.*
  Pronounced "tracks." Conflicts: TRACT (gettract.com, major forestry ERP
  backed by IKEA/BTG Pactual) and TRACES (EU cross-border control system)
  both sit one phoneme away in the same workflow space — confusion guaranteed
  at forestry conferences.

Other pronounceable candidates considered and dropped: BEAM (Apache Beam
collision in the data space), TIMBR/FOSTR (vowel-drop branding reads as
try-hard), JOIST/STAVE (locks to wood-as-construction-material, abandons
energy-feedstock constituency), KEEL (Keel.so backend tool collision).

* Conflict landscape (summary)

| Candidate     | Hardest conflict                                   | Severity |
|---------------+----------------------------------------------------+----------|
| BOOST         | None new (status quo)                              | n/a      |
| B1: GTFT/W/F  | None known; some risk of confusion with GTFBL/GDST | Low      |
| B2: OFDS      | None known                                         | Low      |
| B2: FPDS/DSW  | None known                                         | Low      |
| FORTE         | Forté NZ (PEFC CoC timber flooring company)        | High     |
| TRACS         | TRACT (forestry ERP) + TRACES (EU traceability)    | High     |

* Recommendation (working hypothesis — open to revision through WG discussion)

Rename direct to *Path B2*, with *OFDS — Open Forest Data Standard* as
the leading candidate. Charter amendment (#283) remains the
prerequisite step and lands first.

Reasoning against the other paths:

- /Path A/ doesn't reach the brand-level objection. People still hear
  "Biomass" in conversation no matter what we put in the charter front
  matter; the mental anchor doesn't yield to charter-language. Active
  repositioning (one-pager, crosswalks, visible signals) is fighting
  headwinds, and the hybrid "rename only if signals don't move" path
  defers the decision into a window where M1 GTFT deliverables, charter
  amendment ratification, and EUDR phase-in pressure all need to
  reference the standard by a stable name.
- /Path B1/ (GTF-X pattern) makes BOOST inseparable from the WWF/GTFT
  framework brand, which conflates an open standard with a
  single-sponsor framework — the exact structural posture W3C CG
  open-standard governance is built to avoid. WWF has been explicit
  that it does not intend to own the GTF meta-initiative, but naming
  the standard under the WWF-led umbrella's brand pattern signals
  capture regardless of intent.
- /Path C/ has hard name collisions in the same stakeholder community
  (Forté NZ is a PEFC CoC-certified timber-flooring company; TRACS
  sits one phoneme from TRACT and TRACES) that we cannot recover from
  once the name is published.

Reasoning for B2/OFDS over B2/FPDS or B2/DSW:

- /OFDS/ leads with "Open" — the open-standard governance property is
  visible in the name itself. Important discriminator vs. proprietary
  vendor platforms operating in the same EUDR-compliance space (FSC
  Trace, TRACT, Chainparency, LiveEO TradeAware, osapiens, et al.).
- /OFDS/ takes "Forest" as the scope token, the most inclusive of the
  three (timber, fiber, forest-derived biomass) — preserves the
  three-constituency fit from Decision Criterion 2.
- /FPDS/ (Forest Products Data Standard) is the alternative if WG
  feedback finds "Open" over-engineered or redundant for this audience
  and a plain industry-vocabulary name lands better.
- /DSW/ (Data Standard for Wood) reads thin — drop.

The B2-direct recommendation depends on a structural question
currently open with WWF: whether WWF reads GTFT as the umbrella name
with BOOST occupying a different layer underneath (the reading this
recommendation assumes) or whether WWF wants BOOST-renamed to sit
explicitly as a sibling to GTFBL/GDST. If the latter, the
recommendation re-opens toward Path B1. WWF-side coordination on that
question is in flight separately from this memo.
